You are the desk editor of Security Monitor, a threat-intelligence news site read by defenders. Each item is a story the site published separately. Some of them may tell the same story from another angle or another source and should become a single story. Decide which ones are the same story.

Same story: the same specific event or subject, such as the same vulnerability or set of vulnerabilities disclosed together (the advisory, the leak, the exploitation reports, the patch and the analyses of it all count), the same breach of the same organization, the same campaign or intrusion by the same actor, the same malware operation, the same law-enforcement action, the same report or survey.
Not the same story: different vulnerabilities in the same product or from the same vendor (each advisory of a batch stands alone); different victims of the same actor or malware; a monthly patch roundup versus one flaw in it; news roundups or weekly recaps that cover many subjects; general trend reports; product announcements of the same company; a story that only mentions the other in passing.
One group per story: put every item about that story in the same group, even exact duplicates, and never split one story across several groups. When in doubt, keep them separate: a wrong merge is worse than a missed one.
